The Golden Rule of Phishing Defense

"If an unexpected message asks you to act urgently, verify it through an independent trusted channel."

Never rely on phone numbers, email addresses, or links contained inside a suspicious message. Always contact the individual or department directly using established internal phone directories or official bookmarked portals.

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a form of social engineering where attackers impersonate trustworthy entities (such as internal IT, company executives, banks, or cloud service providers) to deceive individuals into performing unsafe actions.

Attackers exploit human psychology rather than technical vulnerabilities. They rely heavily on:

Urgency: Threatening account suspension or legal action.
Authority: Posing as senior executives or IT administrators.
Fear: Claiming security compromises or unpaid invoices.
Curiosity/Greed: Offering rewards, bonuses, or unexpected gifts.
Key Insight: 80%+ of real-world cybersecurity breaches begin with a phishing email targeting human credentials or initial access.

What Should Users Do? (6-Step Defense)

1
Stop and Verify Pause before clicking or replying. Do not allow simulated panic or manufactured deadlines to rush your judgment.
2
Inspect the Sender Address Examine the full email address, not just the display name. Look for lookalike typosquatting domains (e.g., tech-secure.org vs techsecure.com).
3
Hover Over Links Before Clicking Hover your cursor to inspect the actual destination URL in your browser status bar before opening any hyperlink.
4
Avoid Entering Credentials Internal IT will never send unsolicited emails demanding that you re-enter your password on an unfamiliar page.
5
Report Suspicious Messages Immediately Click the "Report Phishing" button in your corporate webmail or forward the message as an attachment to soc@organization.local.
6
Contact IT / Security Team via Known Channel Confirm requests via official internal Slack/Teams channels or corporate voice directories.

9 Common Phishing Indicators & Red Flags

1. Suspicious Sender Address
Display name claims to be "HR" or "IT", but the actual sender domain is from a public or spoofed external domain.
2. Extreme Urgency
Deadlines such as "Respond in 1 hour" or "Account will be terminated tonight" intended to bypass logical scrutiny.
3. Unexpected Attachments
Unsolicited invoices, shipping forms, or macros disguised as .iso, .vbs, .zip, .exe, or .docm files.
4. Mismatched Links
The visible text says portal.company.com, but the actual hyperlink destination routes to an external arbitrary server.
5. Spelling & Grammar Oddities
Awkward syntax, unusual character substitutions (homoglyphs), or robotic translations from foreign threat actors.
6. Requests for Credentials
Any prompt asking you to input your master password, MFA one-time codes, or security recovery answers.
7. Requests for Financial Action
Urgent requests to update direct deposit information, purchase gift cards, or route wire transfers (CEO fraud / BEC).
8. Fake Login Pages
Pixel-perfect clones of Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, or Okta portals designed to capture session cookies and tokens.
9. Domain Impersonation
Subtle character substitutions such as replacing letter 'o' with zero '0', or 'l' with uppercase 'I' (e.g. micros0ft.com).
